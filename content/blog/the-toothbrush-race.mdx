Brushing teeth used to be a negotiation. Some nights it still is, but not as often, and the reason is a game we made up.

It goes like this. When it's time to brush, we both run to the bathroom, grab a toothbrush and some paste, and race to get every single tooth. We're going for the fastest time, but there's a catch: if you miss a tooth, you lose. So we go fast enough to win and slow enough to be careful, and we check every corner before we call it done.

He always wins. Of course he does. He's three, and he takes the game very seriously. I'm fine with that, because the point was never to beat him. The point was to get the teeth brushed, and the game is working.

The best part is what happens when he resists. I don't argue or count down. I jump up and say, "I'm going to beat you and brush my teeth first!" Then I start pretend running toward the bathroom. He runs after me, or gets his dad to carry him there as fast as possible, so he can beat me to the sink.

I love this game. He gets to win, and he gets to brush. I get the teeth brushed without a fight, and I get to be the one he's racing. We both get exactly what we want.

If you want to try it at home:

1. **Pick a race time.** Bedtime works well. Say it out loud so it feels like a game, not a chore.

2. **Set the toothbrushes out before you start.** A quick start makes the race feel real.

3. **Keep the rule simple.** Miss a tooth, you lose. It makes them slow down and check.

4. **Let them win most of the time.** A small win keeps them wanting to play.

Tomorrow night, we'll race again.
