There's a particular kind of quiet that happens right before it starts. Nico tilts his head. His eyes do this thing. And then it comes.

"But why?"

And friend, once it starts, it does not stop.

## Welcome to the Why Stage

If you've been here, you know. If you haven't, let me paint the picture. Every sentence I say is now a doorway, and on the other side of that doorway is a tiny human with one question and infinite stamina.

"It's time for bed."

"Why?"

"Because your body needs sleep to grow."

"Why?"

"Because that's how little bodies rest and get stronger."

"Why?"

"Because… that's how we're built."

"Why?"

And listen — I try. I give the thorough answer. The clear one. The one with cause and effect and a little science sprinkled in because part of me genuinely loves that he's curious. I want to honor the question. I want to be the mom who explains the world instead of shutting it down with a tired "because I said so."

But here's the honest truth nobody tells you:

> Not every question has an answer.

At some point the whys reach the edge of what I actually know, and I'm standing in the kitchen at the end of a long day trying to explain the fundamental nature of existence to someone who still puts socks on his hands.

## My New Favorite Move

So I've developed a strategy. My go-to. The little verbal aikido of motherhood:

"Why do you think?"

It's beautiful, really. It buys me time. It hands the curiosity back to him. And it turns the interrogation into a conversation.

Except.

Here's what gets me every single time. Especially when I've already answered the question — when I know that he knows, and he knows that I know that he knows — I'll say "why do you think?"

And he looks at me.

And he gives me this little smirk.

And then he tells me the answer. The right one. The one I already gave him. Because he knew it all along.

## He Was Never Confused

That's when it clicked for me. Half of these whys were never about information.

They were about me.

He doesn't always want the explanation. He wants the back-and-forth. He wants my eyes on him, my attention turned fully toward him, the two of us locked in this little loop that's really just another way of saying *stay here with me a little longer.*

The why was never the point. The connection was.

## What I'm Learning

So now when the whys start rolling in, I try to remember that underneath the question is a small person reaching for me. Sometimes he genuinely wants to understand the world. And sometimes he just wants to know that I'll keep showing up to answer — again, and again, and again.

I won't always have the answer. I'll still get tired. I'll still occasionally cave to "because that's just how it is, buddy."

But the smirk? The smirk reminds me that this stage, exhausting as it is, is a gift. He is curious. He is sharp. And he wants me.

So ask away, my love.

But why?

Because I love you. And honestly — that's the only answer I'll never run out of.
