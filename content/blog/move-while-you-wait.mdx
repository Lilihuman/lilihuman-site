I used to work out six days a week, somewhere between one and two hours a day. Right now that feels almost impossible, and I want to be honest about why. We all say we don't have time. But when I look at my days, I do have time. I spend hours lying in bed watching shows, building apps, making things with AI, coming up with ideas, or looking at things to buy and learn. When I finally have a free minute, the energy goes there, not to moving.

My days are full: work, the dog walk, school pickup, dinner, cleaning up, packing for tomorrow, bath time, reading, bedtime. By the time I have a moment for myself, it's about 9 p.m., my body hurts, my head is tired, and all I want is to lie down. I know how much movement matters to me. I've spent years learning how to move safely. I'm still not doing it.

I also live with lupus. When I move regularly, my swelling and muscle seizing are better and my flare-ups are less frequent. When I stop, it gets worse. I've learned that overdoing it backfires too. Every Friday I dance for two hours, and afterwards I can barely walk. I'm not giving that up, but I can't do that six days a week anymore.

So I'm changing the question. Not "how do I fit a workout into my life," but "how do I move through my life?" That's the question I want to help people with, especially those who live with autoimmune conditions or other things that make a full routine hard. The things I've learned over the past few years can help you get movement into your day without it becoming one more chore.

A friend told me she does squats while she brushes her teeth. That's the whole idea, and it stuck with me.

Do the math. Two minutes of brushing is two minutes of squats. Whenever you brush, you can move. Brushing once a day gives you two minutes. Brushing twice a day gives you four. You don't need to change your routine to start.

Most nights I lie down, put on a show, and work on my laptop. A full workout isn't going to happen then. So I've started doing a few abs and some stretching while I watch. I still get to rest and still enjoy the show, but my body isn't stiff from a day of sitting and lying down.

That's when the light went on. Maybe movement doesn't need its own slot. It can fit into what we already do: laundry, dishes, brushing your teeth, waiting for the kettle, waiting on hold, watching a show.

So here's how I'd start. Pick one thing you already do every day and add one movement to it. Laundry is a good first choice: a few squats while you sort, or a calf raise while you fold. Next, add one more, like squats while you brush. Over a few weeks you're not fitting in a workout. You're moving more inside your day.

Keep it safe. Brush with one hand on the counter, and move only on a dry floor. Keep clear of the stove and hot pans while you move. Sit down whenever you need to. If a movement causes pain or a flare, stop and rest, and check with your own health team before you change anything.

The goal is fewer aches, more energy, joints that move more freely, and a habit you don't have to think about. Try one movement tomorrow.
