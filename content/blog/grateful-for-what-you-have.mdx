Happy Thanksgiving to my Canadian friends, and a gentle weekend to everyone else.

Some of the hardest things I do aren't on any job description. They're the expectations I set for myself: the rules about what a good day looks like, and what I have to finish before I'm allowed to feel okay.

One day, Jurie looked over my to-do list and said, "No wonder you feel like you're failing at life."

It had five projects on it, and one of them was to save the world single-handedly. The rest of the list was: clean the whole house, cook five meals, volunteer at the seniors' home, come up with a free workout plan for the community, and then advertise, plan, schedule and run the event.

Then he showed me his. It said things like *get out of bed, brush your teeth, get dressed.*

We laughed, because it was funny. But it also showed me the gap. My list wasn't a list of tasks. It was a measure of whether I was enough, and no day can meet that measure.

I've been setting the bar that high for a long time. Then I fall short, and I tell myself that short means failure. It doesn't. It means the bar was unreasonable.

So this weekend, try a different question. Instead of "What haven't I done yet?", ask:

**Name three things you're grateful for, or three things you didn't have a year ago.**

Here are mine:

1. **My family.** The people who show up, even when I'm not at my best.

2. **My friendships.** Some of them have carried me through more than I've ever said out loud.

3. **Putting myself first, and focusing on my health.** It took me a long time to believe I was allowed to do that.

Your day has the hours it has. Make your plan fit inside them, and notice how much is already there.

*With love, Lili*
